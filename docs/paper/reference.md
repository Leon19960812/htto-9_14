# Related Work and Methodological Positioning

This note synthesizes the historical development and mathematical logic behind the methods we rely on, and positions our contribution within prior work. We emphasize how truss optimization evolved from convex compliance formulations to nonconvex constraints, how sequential convex strategies branched into two major lines (surrogate‑based SPCA vs. physics‑based linearization SCP), and why our design‑dependent hydrostatic setting motivates the latter.

## 1. From Ground Structures to Convex and Nonconvex Models

- Ground structures. Dorn et al. established the finite ground‑structure paradigm for truss design, enabling algorithmic search over a fixed candidate set \cite{dorn1964automatic}.
- Convex compliance. Ben‑Tal and Nemirovski showed that compliance‑based truss topology can be cast as a convex conic program (SDP/CQP) by exploiting affine dependence of stiffness on areas \cite{ben1997robust}. This line yields strong theory and reliable numerics when loads and geometry are fixed.
- Beyond compliance: geometry and state constraints. Introducing geometric variables (node positions) or state constraints (displacements/stresses) breaks convexity: (i) element directions and lengths become nonlinear in geometry; (ii) many stress/displacement constraints induce nonconvex forms. As a result, sequential convex methodologies became central.

## 2. The SCP Lineage: From MMA to Convergence‑Controlled SCP

- MMA and convex approximations. Svanberg’s Method of Moving Asymptotes introduced reciprocal‑type convex surrogates for nonconvex structural objectives/constraints.
- SCP formalization. Zillober \cite{zillober1993globally} placed these ideas into a convergence‑controlled sequential convex programming framework (merit/line‑search), establishing global convergence for broad classes. Ni–Zillober–Schittkowski \cite{ni2005sequential} pushed scalable implementations (SCPIP) for large FEM systems.
- Mathematical logic. The core loop is: build a convex subproblem near the current iterate (via local models or surrogates), solve it robustly, and regulate step size by trust‑region/merit to maintain progress.

## 3. SPCA: Parametric Convex Overestimates with Touching Conditions

- Idea. Beck–Ben‑Tal–Tetruashvili \cite{beck2009} proposed Sequential Parametric Convex Approximation (SPCA): replace each nonconvex constraint \(g(x)\) by a parameterized convex upper bound \(G(x,y)\) that matches value and gradient at the current iterate (touching conditions). This preserves feasibility and ensures a monotone objective sequence; under mild assumptions, accumulation points satisfy KKT conditions.
- Application scope. SPCA is particularly effective for truss problems with design‑independent loads and nonconvex stress/displacement constraints, where suitable conic reformulations of \(G\) lead to efficient CQP subproblems.

## 4. Our Problem Class: Design‑Dependent Hydrostatics and Nonaffine LMI

- Coupled loading. In our setting, the load vector \(\mathbf{f}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\) is generated by a shell–truss interaction model under hydrostatic pressure (softmax‑mapped supports), hence depends on geometry; stiffness \(\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{A},\boldsymbol{\theta})\) is also geometry‑dependent.
- Nonaffine Schur complement. Both \(\mathbf{K}\) and \(\mathbf{f}\) enter a Schur complement LMI nonaffinely, making it nonconvex in \((\mathbf{A},\boldsymbol{\theta})\). Constructing global convex upper bounds that remain informative for this coupled, nonaffine matrix dependence is nontrivial in practice.

## 5. Why SCP with First‑Order Linearization and Trust Region

- Physics‑faithful local models. We linearize \(\mathbf{K}\) and \(\mathbf{f}\) at the iterate using explicit derivatives (including shell‑induced load Jacobians), retaining a tight, interpretable model of the mechanics.
- Convex subproblems as SDP. The linearized Schur block is affine in the decisions, so each subproblem is a well‑posed SDP, solvable by mature interior‑point solvers.
- Step control and smoothing. A trust‑region and load continuation scheme mitigates model invalidation due to load–geometry coupling; softmax support mapping ensures differentiability of the load path.
- Trade‑off. Compared to SPCA’s global overestimation (feasible/monotone guarantees), our SCP design favors implementability and physical fidelity under design‑dependent loading, while still adhering to “sequential convexification” with explicit model management.

## 6. Positioning and Takeaways

- Historical arc. From convex compliance (strong theory) to nonconvex constraints (stress/geometry), the community adopted two complementary strategies: (i) surrogate‑based SPCA with touching upper bounds and feasibility/monotonicity; (ii) physics‑based SCP with linearization plus step control.
- Problem‑driven choice. For shell–truss coupled, design‑dependent hydrostatics yielding nonaffine LMI, we prioritize the latter to keep subproblems close to the governing equations and to leverage SDP structure.
- Outlook. Incorporating SPCA‑style conservative envelopes locally (e.g., selective upper bounds or proximal regularization on sensitive blocks) is a promising extension, but falls beyond the scope of this paper’s implementation.

References central to this discussion include Zillober \cite{zillober1993globally}, Ni–Zillober–Schittkowski \cite{ni2005sequential}, and Beck–Ben‑Tal–Tetruashvili \cite{beck2009}, together with classic ground‑structure and convex compliance formulations \cite{dorn1964automatic,ben1997robust}.
