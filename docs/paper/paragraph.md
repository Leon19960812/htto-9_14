Fig.\ref{fig:area_distribution_comparison} complements Table \ref{tab:compliance_comparison} by showing how the cross-sectional areas redistribute under each discretisation. The compliance gains reported in the table (49.7\% at eight sectors down to 15.2\% at twenty sectors) track how aggressively SCP can reposition material into high-capacity members.

For the coarse layouts (8--12 sectors) the contrast is clear. The fixed-geometry SDP reallocates less aggressively: 41\% and 40\% of the active members exceed \(8{,}000\ \mathrm{mm}^2\) in the 8- and 12-sector cases, leaving a sizable contingent in the mid-range bins. SCP prunes many of those moderate members via node motion and merging, and raises the fractions to 86\% and 65\%, respectively, which is consistent with the large compliance reductions reported in Table \ref{tab:compliance_comparison}.

As the sector count increases (16--20) the histograms begin to overlap, mirroring the smaller improvements in Table \ref{tab:compliance_comparison}. Nevertheless, SCP continues to simplify the network: node merging cuts the final member count from 298 to 234 at 16 sectors and from 374 to 207 at 20 sectors, while roughly half of the remaining members still lie in the \(>8{,}000\ \mathrm{mm}^2\) bin and the total material usage remains comparable or lower (e.g. 0.887 vs. 1.001 m^2 at 16 sectors; 0.708 vs. 0.940 m^2 at 20 sectors). Thus, even when the SDP layout is already close to optimal, the SCP pipeline maintains a slimmer, more targeted structure and preserves a modest compliance advantage.
